PART 1 — CORE CONCEPT & DESIGN PILLARS
1. Genre & Platform
Genre: Exploration-Adventure / Functional Base-Building Hybrid
Platform: Roblox — PC, mobile, console, and tablet.
2. Player Role
The player is the Chief Expedition Officer & Deep-Sea Submarine Pilot.
The player operates a bioluminescent deep-sea research station and
pilots submarines into handcrafted underwater zones.
3. Tone
^(●) Wonder and curiosity first.
^(●) Eerie and mysterious at greater depths, without becoming horror.
^(●) The ocean should feel unknown, beautiful, and slightly unsettling.
^(●) Discovery should be the primary emotional reward.
4. Unique Selling Proposition
The station is a functional operational base, not a passive cash
printer.
The player prepares at the station, dives into the ocean, makes
meaningful decisions, returns with discoveries, processes those
discoveries, and upgrades the station/submarine to reach deeper areas.
5. Core Design Pillars
1. Active Exploration — The dive itself is the main game.
2. Purpose-Driven Base Expansion — Station wings directly support
exploration.
3. Fair Risk — Failure costs a portion of unsecured cargo, never
permanent progression.
4. Observation & Preparation Over RNG — Knowledge and equipment matter
more than luck.
5. Wonder First — Exploration and discovery are the emotional core.
6. Permanent Station Ownership — No rebirth/reset prestige.
6. Core Loop
PLAN → DIVE → DECIDE → DEBRIEF → UPGRADE → GO DEEPER
^(●) PLAN: Choose submarine, equipment, objective, and cargo protection.
^(●) DIVE: Pilot the submarine through handcrafted underwater
environments.
^(●) DECIDE: Continue deeper or return based on Battery/Oxygen, Hull,
Cargo, hazards, and discoveries.
^(●) DEBRIEF: Process cargo, earn Credits and Research Data, update the
Codex.
^(●) UPGRADE: Unlock new capabilities through the station and research
systems.
^(●) GO DEEPER: Use new capabilities to reach new areas and discover
more.
7. Session Structures
^(●) Short: approximately 10–15 minutes.
^(●) Medium: approximately 30–45 minutes.
^(●) Long: approximately 60–90+ minutes.
8. Two-Currency Rule
The game has exactly two gameplay currencies:
^(●) Credits — primary economic currency.
^(●) Research Data — earned only through active exploration, scanning,
studying, and decoding.
Research Data cannot be purchased with Robux, earned passively, or
obtained through AFK systems.
9. Anti-Goals
Deep Lantern is not:
^(●) A generic button tycoon.
^(●) An idle/AFK game.
^(●) A pure RNG game.
^(●) A frustrating survival simulator.
^(●) Pay-to-win.
^(●) A spreadsheet-heavy inventory simulator.
^(●) Jumpscare horror.
^(●) Forced multiplayer.
^(●) A rebirth/reset game.
^(●) A massive MMO-scale open world.
10. North Star
> Prepare at your home base, descend into an awe-inspiring unknown, make
smart decisions under pressure, and bring your discoveries back to the
light.

PART 2 — PROGRESSION, ECONOMY & LONG-TERM STRUCTURE
1. Progression Philosophy
Progression focuses on capability over stat inflation.
Players should unlock new ways to explore rather than repeatedly receive
tiny numerical increases.
Progression has four major vectors:
1. Depth Reach
2. Station Tier
3. Codex Mastery
4. Submarine Fleet
2. World / Depth Zones
The planned depth progression consists of:
1. Shallows
2. Kelp Forest
3. Twilight Reef
4. Abyss
5. Lost Ruins
6. The Hollow
7. Beyond
Exact numerical depth thresholds are intentionally deferred until
technical balancing.
3. Station Tiers
Station progression:
Outpost → Station → Complex → Citadel → Deep Empire
Citadel and Deep Empire are post-MVP progression.
4. Submarine Progression
Submarine progression uses:
^(●) Hull frame.
^(●) Utility/navigation slots.
^(●) Internal/module slots.
^(●) Capability-focused equipment.
Exact module statistics and recipes are deferred to technical
implementation.
5. Submarine Classes
Planned classes:
^(●) Scout
^(●) Hauler
^(●) Surveyor
^(●) Abyssal
^(●) Multi-Crew — future/deferred detailed implementation.
6. Capability-Based Upgrades
Examples include:
^(●) Plasma Cutter.
^(●) Thermal Recharging Unit.
^(●) Acoustic Decoy Launcher.
Upgrades should unlock capabilities rather than produce endless +1% stat
inflation.
7. Economy

Research Data
Research Data is earned through:
^(●) Scanning.

^(●) Verification.
^(●) Active exploration.
Research Data cannot be:
^(●) Purchased with Robux.

^(●) Traded/transferred between players.
8. Physical Resources
Main physical resource categories:
^(●) Geological Minerals.
^(●) Biological Samples.

^(●) Field Consumables.
9. Technology Tree
Three research branches:
1. Engineering
2. Survey

Endgame includes:

PART 3 — WORLD, ZONES, EXPLORATION & DIVE DESIGN
1. World Philosophy
Small, Beautiful, Playable First
The world uses a compact, handcrafted, vertical environment.
Scale is created through:
^(●) Dense level design.
^(●) Narrow passages.
^(●) Fog.
^(●) Darkness.
^(●) Depth transitions.
^(●) Modular reusable assets.
The game should avoid expensive physics, fluid simulation, and
continuous complex AI.
2. World Structure
Compact Station → Launch/Dock Chute → Primary Zone → Sub-Zones → Points
of Interest → Secrets/Gates → Depth Transitions
The environment should be modular and reusable.
3. Exploration Philosophy
Exploration is player-led.
Navigation uses:
^(●) Visual signposting.
^(●) Simple sonar.
^(●) Environmental clues.
^(●) Landmarks.
^(●) Depth information.
No quest-marker GPS system.
4. MVP Dive Loop
PLAN → DIVE → EXPLORE / COLLECT / SCAN → DECIDE / RETURN → DEBRIEF /
UPGRADE
5. Zone Framework
Each zone should define:
^(●) Core fantasy.
^(●) Visual identity.
^(●) Exploration mechanic.
^(●) Hazard.
^(●) Discoveries.
^(●) Resources.
^(●) Biological identity.
^(●) Secrets.
^(●) Return value.
^(●) Set-piece.
6. MVP Zones
Launch MVP targets:
1. Station.
2. Shallows.
3. Kelp Forest.
4. Twilight Reef.
7. Post-MVP Zones
Future:
^(●) Abyss.
^(●) Lost Ruins.
^(●) The Hollow.
^(●) Beyond.
8. Hazards
Hazards use:
^(●) Static trigger volumes.
^(●) Simple state checks.
^(●) Readable feedback.
No complex fluid or structural simulation.
9. Creature Performance
Creatures use:
^(●) Small groups.
^(●) Simple waypoint/spline/state AI.
^(●) Distance-based activation.
^(●) Lightweight rigs.
No predator/prey ecosystem simulation.
10. Visual Quality
Visual quality comes primarily from:
^(●) Lighting.
^(●) Fog.
^(●) Atmosphere.
^(●) Emissive materials.
^(●) Carefully selected particles.
Not from massive meshes or huge particle counts.
11. Reusable Modular Assets
Assets should be designed as reusable kits.
The principle is reusable asset variety, not creating thousands of
simultaneously active objects.
12. Deferred Systems
Deferred:
^(●) Tractor Winch.
^(●) Hardwired Compass.
^(●) Complex ecosystem.
^(●) Dynamic weather.
^(●) Large procedural generation.
^(●) NPC fleets.
^(●) Continuous currents.
13. Performance Rules
^(●) Fake scale instead of simulating massive environments.
^(●) Use triggers instead of expensive simulation.
^(●) Modularize everything.
^(●) Use lighting for atmosphere.
^(●) Keep AI simple but visually effective.
14. MVP Core Systems
^(●) Simplified submarine piloting.
^(●) Inventory/cargo.
^(●) Static resource nodes.
^(●) Basic scanning.
^(●) Lighting/spotlights.
^(●) Safe return/respawn.
^(●) Partial unsecured-cargo loss on failure.
Failure does not destroy permanent progression.
